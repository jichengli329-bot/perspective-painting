# Visual foundation brief

## Intent

The first screenshot must resemble a deliberately designed optical toy, not a Unity mechanics prototype. It is a composition and material test, not a gameplay milestone.

## Composition

- 16:9 landscape frame.
- Orthographic camera at a calm three-quarter angle, looking slightly downward.
- Main construction area occupies the left/center two-thirds.
- Projection board stands in the right/rear third and remains readable.
- Use generous negative space and avoid filling the frame with grids or debug lines.

## Form language

- Softly beveled or rounded block forms.
- A shallow raised plinth with rounded edges.
- Thin structural supports for the projection board.
- No raw primitives presented as finished objects.

## Palette

- Background/environment: warm ivory, approximately `#F2EEE6`.
- Stage/plinth: light warm stone, approximately `#DED7CA`.
- Interactive construction pieces: muted teal, approximately `#4C9A92`.
- Accent: soft coral or amber, approximately `#E58A65`.
- Projection surface: near-white with a dark desaturated blue-gray frame.

Exact values may be tuned for lighting, but color relationships should remain restrained and cohesive.

## Lighting and rendering

- Soft directional key with broad, readable contact shadows.
- Gentle cool or neutral fill so teal pieces retain shape in shadow.
- Ambient occlusion should clarify contact, not dirty the image.
- Avoid strong bloom, chromatic aberration, extreme vignette, or glossy plastic glare.
- Prefer subtle specular response and moderate roughness.

## Review questions

1. Does the frame look like a product rather than an editor demo?
2. Is the intended play area obvious without a grid overlay?
3. Are pieces pleasant and tactile enough to invite interaction?
4. Is the projection board visually connected to the construction stage?
5. Can future target/current/overlap feedback fit without cluttering the composition?
